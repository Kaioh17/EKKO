# Finance knowledge

Stable background concepts only -- no live prices, no "current" market
data, no specific ticker recommendations. If asked about live data,
answer from what's already in the conversation (e.g. a briefing that
already mentioned a stock is up) and offer to look up more via `urls`,
never invent a number.

## Core concepts, in plain terms

- **Emergency fund**: cash set aside for unplanned expenses (job loss,
  car repair, medical bill), usually before any investing. A common
  rule of thumb is 3-6 months of essential expenses, but even a small
  fund (one month, or less) is meaningfully better than none.
- **Brokerage account**: an account used to buy/sell investments like
  stocks or funds. Opening one is a real step with real paperwork --
  never treat it as something that happens automatically or in the
  background.
- **Index fund**: a fund that holds a broad basket of stocks (e.g.
  tracking the S&P 500) instead of picking individual companies. Lower
  effort and typically lower risk than picking single stocks, though
  still subject to market swings.
- **401(k) / employer retirement account**: a workplace retirement
  account, often with an employer match -- if a match exists and isn't
  being used, that's usually worth mentioning before anything else,
  since it's close to free money.
- **Roth IRA / IRA**: individual retirement accounts with different tax
  treatment than a regular brokerage account; relevant mainly once
  someone has income and is thinking past just an emergency fund.
- **High-interest debt**: credit cards and similar debt, typically at a
  much higher rate than likely investment returns -- paying this down is
  usually a stronger move than investing the same money, when both are
  on the table.
- **Dollar-cost averaging**: investing a fixed amount on a regular
  schedule rather than all at once, which is a way to talk about
  *habit* and *timing* without ever naming a specific dollar figure for
  someone (see guardrails).
